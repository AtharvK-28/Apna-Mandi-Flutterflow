## 1. Executive Summary

**Company:** ShilpMandi ("Artisan Marketplace")

**Elevator pitch:** ShilpMandi is a mobile-first ecosystem built for India's decentralized artisan and handicraft workforce. It connects independent craftspeople, weavers, raw-material suppliers, apprentices, and global consumers. By digitizing the fragmented handicraft economy, we solve four critical problems: hiring temporary workshop help, trading surplus raw materials, procuring bulk supplies without exploitative middlemen, and monetizing generations-old heritage designs.

**Problem:** India's 7+ million artisans operate in highly fragmented, cash-reliant micro-businesses. They lack digital tools to manage supply chains, find skilled labor for large orders, and often lose 60-80% of their product value to middlemen. 

**Solution:** A unified platform with four core modules:
- **Sahayak Connect** — On-demand gig marketplace for finding skilled apprentices and helpers (e.g., finding a dye-mixer, an extra loom operator, or a finishing specialist for a bulk order).
- **Material Exchange** — Peer-to-peer barter and trade for surplus raw materials (yarn, clay, natural dyes) among local artisan clusters to reduce waste and lower costs.
- **Parampara (Heritage)** — A storytelling-driven D2C module that allows master artisans to license their unique, often GI-tagged (Geographical Indication) designs directly to brands or sell authenticated products globally.
- **Direct Procurement** — Vendor-to-supplier portal for sourcing raw materials directly from manufacturers, bypassing predatory middlemen.

**Target market:** India's handicraft and handloom sector, which employs over 7 million artisans, heavily concentrated in regional clusters (e.g., Varanasi, Kutch, Jaipur).

## 2. Problem Statement

The Indian handicraft sector is the second-largest employment generator after agriculture, yet it remains technologically underserved:

1. **Labor bottlenecks:** Artisans receive bulk corporate or export orders but cannot fulfill them because finding vetted, temporary skilled labor (like a block printer or embroiders) is entirely reliant on slow word-of-mouth.
2. **Raw material inefficiencies:** An artisan might have 5kg of expensive surplus silk yarn, while a neighbor is halting production due to a shortage of the exact same material. There is no local digital network to match this.
3. **Exploitative supply chains:** Artisans buy raw materials (threads, dyes, wood) from local aggregators at high markups due to lack of direct access to wholesale manufacturers.
4. **Stolen IP and lost legacy:** Generational motifs and techniques are frequently copied by power-loom factories. Artisans have no platform to legally timestamp, brand, and monetize their unique ancestral designs (IP) directly to conscious consumers.
5. **No formal business footprint:** Without digital transaction histories, artisans remain "invisible" to formal banking, making it impossible to secure working capital loans at fair interest rates.

**Why now:** Rising smartphone penetration in rural/semi-urban clusters, growing global demand for ethical and sustainable fashion/decor, and government initiatives like ONDC (Open Network for Digital Commerce) create the perfect tailwind for a digital artisan ecosystem.

## 3. Solution / Product

ShilpMandi is a modular platform offering role-based access for **Artisans (Makers)**, **Sahayaks (Helpers/Gig Workers)**, and **Suppliers**, alongside a D2C storefront.

### Sahayak Connect
A localized gig-board for craft clusters. If a potter gets a massive Diwali order, they can post a gig for "3 clay-preparers for 5 days." Local semi-skilled workers can apply. It solves the seasonal capacity constraint.

### Material Exchange
A localized trading post for raw materials. Artisans can sell, trade, or pool resources to buy in bulk. E.g., Five independent weavers can pool their demand on the app to order a wholesale batch of cotton yarn, bringing their individual costs down by 30%.

### Parampara (Heritage Commerce)
An IP-protection and premium sales channel. Master artisans can mint their unique designs (with video proof of the traditional process). Consumers or fashion labels can buy the authentic product or license the design, ensuring the original creator gets royalties. It turns a physical craft into a scalable brand.

### Direct Procurement
A B2B marketplace connecting artisans directly to factories producing raw materials (yarn mills, dye manufacturers, tools), featuring transparent pricing, group-buying features, and tracked delivery.

## 4. Unique Value Proposition (USP)

- **Holistic Ecosystem vs. Pure E-commerce:** Platforms like Etsy or Amazon Karigar only help artisans *sell*. ShilpMandi helps them *build, source, and hire*, attacking the supply-side friction.
- **Hyper-local Cluster Focus:** The app is designed around India's geographical craft clusters (e.g., all block printers in Bagru), making peer-to-peer exchange and local gig work highly feasible.
- **IP Monetization for the Unorganized Sector:** Creating a royalty and licensing model for traditional art forms, which is unprecedented in the informal sector.
- **Data-Driven Financial Inclusion:** By routing procurement and gig payments through the app, ShilpMandi builds a verified cash-flow history, enabling future integrations for micro-lending.

## 5. Target Market (TAM / SAM / SOM)

- **TAM (Total Addressable Market):** The Indian Handicraft Market is valued at approx. $3.5 Billion and employs ~7 million artisans. 
- **SAM (Serviceable Addressable Market):** Artisans in major, digitally-connected geographical clusters (approx. 2-3 million artisans in states like UP, Gujarat, Rajasthan).
- **SOM (Serviceable Obtainable Market):** Year 1 pilot targeting 10,000 artisans in 3 specific textile clusters (e.g., Varanasi silk weavers, Kutch embroiderers, Jaipur block printers).

## 6. Competitor Analysis

| Competitor | Focus Area | Gap ShilpMandi Fills |
|---|---|---|
| **Etsy / Amazon Karigar** | D2C Global Sales | Purely sales-focused. They do not help with sourcing raw materials, hiring labor, or trading surplus. |
| **Udaan / IndiaMART** | B2B Wholesale | Generic B2B. Not tailored to the specific micro-needs of traditional artisans (e.g., specific organic dyes). |
| **Local Middlemen** | End-to-end supply & sales | High exploitation (taking 60%+ margins). ShilpMandi replaces them with transparent, flat-fee digital connections. |

**Whitespace:** ShilpMandi is the only "Operating System" for an artisan's workshop, handling the backend (supply/labor) so the artisan can focus on the craft.

## 7. Go-to-Market (GTM) Strategy

1. **Cluster-by-Cluster Launch:** Launch exclusively in one handicraft cluster (e.g., Maheshwar handlooms) to build dense local liquidity for the Material Exchange and Sahayak Connect.
2. **NGO & Cooperative Partnerships:** Partner with existing craft NGOs, self-help groups, and cooperatives for trusted, bulk onboarding.
3. **Hero Artisans:** Onboard National Award-winning artisans first for the *Parampara* module to create aspirational value.
4. **B2B Supply Seeding:** Pre-onboard raw material suppliers by aggregating demand manually during the pilot phase to guarantee cheaper prices on day one.

---

## 8. Business Model / Revenue Streams

1. **SaaS / Subscription:** A low-cost monthly subscription (e.g., ₹99/month) for advanced portfolio tools and group-buying capabilities.
2. **Marketplace Commissions:** 
   - 3-5% take-rate on raw material procurement (charged to the supplier).
   - 10-15% commission on D2C premium sales via the Parampara module.
3. **Licensing Royalties:** 20% cut on any B2B design licensing deals brokered through the platform.
4. **Fintech (Future):** Lead generation fees for connecting artisans with NBFCs for micro-loans based on their platform data.
